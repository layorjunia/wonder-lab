=== physical-forces

--- 🔨

- The feather floated away
+ They hit the dust together
hint: Look at the fact box. It tells you what happened with no air in the way.
explain: With no air resistance, both the hammer and the feather hit the ground at the same time.

--- 🪶
p: On Earth, a feather falls slower than a hammer because of air.
fact: Air pushes back harder the faster you fall and the more spread out you are. When the push equals your weight, you stop speeding up. A skydiver levels off at about 120 mph.
try: Drop two identical sheets of paper, one flat and one screwed into a ball. Same paper, same weight, but they will fall very differently.
check: Why does a feather fall slower than a hammer on Earth?
+ The air pushes back on it
- Gravity speeds up a hammer more than a feather
- The feather has no weight at all
hint: Look at the fact box. It explains how air affects falling objects.
explain: Air resistance slows down the feather because it pushes back harder, especially on something spread out like a feather.

--- 🛷
p: A moving thing keeps moving in a straight line unless something pushes it.
fact: On Earth, friction and air are always pushing back. But on ice or in space, you can see it plainly.
try: Slide a book across a smooth floor and then across a rug. Notice how the rug makes it stop faster.
try: Put a card on a glass and a coin on the card. Flick the card sideways hard. The coin does not come with it — it drops straight into the glass.

--- 🔥
p: Friction stops everything by turning movement into heat.
fact: Brake discs glow hot, and rubbing your hands warms them up. Rough surfaces make more friction, but so do some very smooth ones — two flat sheets of glass grip surprisingly hard.

check: What does friction turn movement into?
- Wind
+ Heat
- Cold
hint: Look at the first fact on the page to see what happens when you rub your hands.
explain: Friction turns movement into heat, which is why rubbing your hands together warms them up.

--- ⚖️
p: A lever is a simple machine that trades distance for force.
term: lever = A tool that lets you push or pull one end to move something else with more force at the other end.
fact: Push the far end of a long bar a long way, and the near end moves a short way but with much more force. Scissors, nutcrackers, wheelbarrows, and a seesaw are all levers.
check: What does a lever trade?
- Weight for speed
- Time for speed
+ Distance for force
hint: Look at the first fact on the page to see how the ends of a lever move differently.
explain: A lever moves one end a long way to get more force at the other end.

--- 🚀
p: Every push comes with an equal push the other way.
fact: A rocket does not push against the air — it throws gas out of the back, and the gas pushes the rocket forward.
p: That is why rockets work better in space. With nothing in the way, the gas still pushes the rocket forward without any resistance.
check: What pushes a rocket forward?
- The pull of the moon
+ The gas it throws out the back
- The air pushing against the rocket
hint: Look at the first fact on the page to see what the rocket throws out.
explain: The gas that comes out the back pushes the rocket forward.

--- 🌙
p: A hammer and a feather landing together. A brake glowing hot. A lever lifting something heavy. A rocket leaving the ground.
p: Four different things, and every one of them follows the same few rules about pushes and motion.

=== physical-light

--- ☀️

--- 🌈

term: prism = A piece of glass or clear material that spreads light into its colors.

check: What did Newton's second prism do?
- Turned the colors into shades of gray
+ Put the colors back into white light
- Split each color into many smaller colors

--- 🌅
p: The sky is blue because air scatters short waves, like blue, much more strongly than long ones, like red.

check: Why is the sky blue?
- Air scatters red light more strongly

+ Air scatters blue light more strongly
hint: Look at the fact box to see how air affects different colors of light.

--- 🪞
p: When light hits a mirror, it bounces off at exactly the same angle it arrived, like a ball hitting a wall.
fact: This one rule explains how every mirror, periscope, and reflector works.
try: Shine a flashlight at a mirror from a slant in a dark room with a little dust or steam in the air so the beam shows. Change the angle and watch the reflection change by the same amount.

--- 🥛
p: Light does something surprising when it crosses from air into water.
fact: Light bends as it moves between air and water. That's why a pencil in a glass of water looks broken, and why a pool is deeper than it seems.
try: Stand a pencil in a glass of water and look at it from the side. You'll see the pencil appear to bend or break.
check: Why does a pencil in water look broken?

explain: Light bends as it moves between water and air, making the pencil look broken.

--- 🎨
p: Mixing light and mixing paint are opposites.
fact: Red, green, and blue light together make white. But red, green, and blue paint together make a muddy brown because each paint takes colors away rather than adding them.
try: Put a drop of water on a phone screen showing a white picture and look closely. You will see the red, green, and blue stripes the screen is really made of.
check: What do red, green, and blue paint make together?
+ A muddy brown
- A bright yellow
- A beautiful purple
hint: Look at the fact box. It explains how mixing paints takes colors away.
explain: Red, green, and blue paint together make a muddy brown because each paint is taking colors away.

--- 🔦
p: A sun that is eight minutes old. White light split by a prism. A blue sky that turns red at sunset. A mirror, a pencil in water, a box of paints.
p: One light, and so many ways to watch it change.

=== physical-sound
idea: Sound is a journey that can be fast or slow, loud or high-pitched. Discover how to measure distance with an echo and why children hear higher pitches than adults.

--- 🔔

- Air
+ Steel
- Water

--- 🍷

p: Ships find the seabed this way and bats find moths.
check: How do you measure distance with an echo?

hint: Look at the fact box. It explains the steps to calculate the distance.
explain: You time how long it takes for the sound to return, halve that time, and then multiply by the speed of sound to get the distance.

--- 🎧
p: Sound needs something to travel through. It moves faster in water and faster still in steel. Everything has a note it likes to ring at, and sometimes the sound comes back as an echo.
p: So next time you hear a sound, think about its trip, its shake, and maybe even its return.
