=== animals-mammals-1
idea: Five animals, each built for one job. Find out who sprints at 65 miles an hour, who vanishes on bare rock, and who hears dinner under the sand.
game: Cheetah = The fastest runner on land | Snow leopard = The ghost of the mountains | Tiger = The largest cat in the world | Lion = The only cat that lives in a pride | Fennec fox = The smallest fox, with enormous ears

--- 🐾

p: Each one is built for a different job.

--- 🐾 @cheetah
p: Start with the fastest runner on land. The fastest cheetah ever timed ran 100 meters in 5.95 seconds. The human world record is 9.58.
fact(The race): Her name was Sarah and she lived at the Cincinnati Zoo. She chased a fluffy lure on a rope and hit a peak of 61 mph (98 km/h).
p: Cheetah speeds get exaggerated all the time. The highest reliably measured is 65 mph (104 km/h). A famous figure of 71 mph came from a faulty way of measuring and was thrown out.
check: What is the highest speed ever reliably measured for a cheetah?
- 71 mph
- 35 mph
+ 65 mph
hint: Look at the last two sentences. One number was thrown out.
explain: 65 mph. The famous 71 came from a faulty way of measuring.

--- 🐾 @snow-leopard
p: Now climb. The snow leopard lives higher up the mountains than almost any other large animal, between about 9,800 and 14,800 feet (3,000 to 4,500 m).
p: Its tail is up to 3 feet (1 m) long, nearly as long as the rest of the cat. On a ledge barely wider than a paw, it works as a balance bar.
term: rosette = A dark smudge shaped like a ring. A snow leopard's rosettes break up its outline, so on bare rock people cannot find the cat.
fact(The ghost of the mountains): Researchers can work in snow leopard country for months and see nothing but tracks. Almost everything we know comes from camera traps and GPS collars, not from watching.

--- 🐾 @tiger
p: No cat alive is bigger than the tiger. A large male weighs around 485 lb (220 kg), about as much as three grown men.
fact(Stripes to the skin): Shave a tiger and the stripes are still there, because the pattern is in the skin itself. Every tiger's pattern is its own. Even the left and right sides of one tiger do not match.
p: Orange looks loud to you, but deer cannot see orange. The best read is that in dry grass the stripes look like broken lines of shadow.
check: How can researchers tell one tiger from another?
- By the sound of its roar
+ By its stripes
- By how much it weighs
hint: Look at the box about the stripes. It says whose pattern is whose.
explain: By its stripes. The pattern is in the skin and no two tigers share one.

--- 🐾 @lion
p: The lion is the only cat that lives in a family group, called a pride.
fact(The pride): A pride is a permanent group of mostly related females. Grandmothers, mothers, sisters and daughters stay together for life.
fact(The roar): A lion's roar carries about 5 miles (8 km) across open country. The low pitch is the trick, because deep sound bends around trees and rocks.
p: And a lion may rest 20 hours out of every 24.

--- 🐾 @fennec-fox
p: The smallest fox on earth has the biggest ears for its size. A fennec weighs about 3 lb (1.3 kg), and its ears are nearly 4 inches (10 cm) tall.
fact(Hunting by listening): A fennec stops dead and swivels its head. Then it digs straight down into open sand and comes up with a beetle or a lizard. Its ears pick up small movements underground.
p: It can live where there is nothing to drink, because it takes its water from the animals it eats.
check: How does a fennec fox find food hidden in the sand?
+ It listens for it
- It smells it from far away
- It follows tracks
hint: Look at the box. It says what the fox does with its ears.
explain: It listens. Its ears pick up small movements underground, and it digs to the exact spot.

--- 📊
p: Now put the five side by side. The tiger weighs about 485 lb and the lion about 419. The cheetah and the snow leopard each weigh about 99 lb. The fennec fox weighs about 3.
fact(Speed): The cheetah's top speed is 65 mph. The tiger and the lion both manage about 35 mph, and only for a short sprint.
check: Which of the five is the heaviest?
- The lion
- The cheetah
+ The tiger
hint: Look at the first paragraph and find the biggest number.
explain: The tiger, at about 485 lb.

--- 🐾
p: A cheetah that sprints, a snow leopard that hides, a tiger with stripes in its skin, a lion with a family and a fox that listens.
p: Five animals, five different jobs, and every one of them built for its own.

quiz: Which animal is the fastest runner on land?

- The lion
explain: The cheetah, with a highest measured speed of 65 mph.

- To warn other cats of danger
+ To balance on narrow ledges
- To swim across mountain rivers
explain: The tail works as a balance bar on ledges barely wider than a paw.
quiz: Where are a tiger's stripes?

explain: A pride is a permanent group of mostly related females and their young.
quiz: How does a fennec fox find food under the sand?

explain: Its ears pick up small movements underground, and it digs to the exact spot.

p: Each one lives in its own world.

--- 🐾 @arctic-fox
p: Meet the arctic fox, a small fox with deep fur that lives farther north than almost any other land animal.
fact(Extra-furry feet): Turn one paw over and even the soles are furred, pads and all. Its scientific name, Vulpes lagopus, means "hare-foot" — no other wild dog has this.

--- 🐾 @grey-wolf
p: Meet the gray wolf, the largest wild member of the dog family. It has long legs and hunts in a family group.
term: carnassials = Sharp teeth shaped like blades that cut through hide, tendon, and bone.
fact(The pack is a family): A wolf pack is made up of parents and pups from this year and last. The idea of an "alpha" wolf fighting for dominance comes from watching zoo wolves, not those in the wild.
p: Wolves hunt with their noses. Researchers have seen them head straight for a moose 1.5 miles (2.4 km) away, downwind and out of sight.

--- 🐾 @polar-bear
p: Next up is the polar bear, the largest bear on earth. It's a white-coated hunter that lives out on Arctic sea ice and hunts seals.
fact(The bear that swims far): One collared female swam for nine days straight across 426 miles (687 km) of open Arctic sea. She lost about a fifth of her body weight doing it.
p: Polar bear fur isn't white. Each hair is actually a clear, hollow tube that scatters sunlight in all directions, making the fur look white.
check: What makes polar bear fur appear white?
- Black skin under the fur
+ Clear, hollow tubes that scatter sunlight
- Thick fur padding on the soles of its feet
hint: Look at the paragraph about the hollow hair and black skin.
explain: The hollow core of each hair scatters sunlight in all directions, making the fur look white.

--- 🐾 @grizzly-bear
p: Next, meet the grizzly bear, a large brown bear of North America's mountains and salmon rivers. It has a hump of muscle on its shoulders and claws built for digging.
fact(The digging champion): The hump over its shoulders is solid muscle, powering the front legs to dig through ground you would need a pickaxe for. Its claws are 2-3.9 in (5-10 cm) long and almost straight, perfect for turning over rocks and hauling out roots and ground squirrels.
p: By late summer, a grizzly goes into hyperphagia — "over-eating." It feeds up to 20 hours a day.
p: During this time, it can put on 3-7 lb (1.5-3 kg) of fat every single day. This is crucial for surviving the long winter in its den.

--- 🐾 @giant-panda
p: Next up is the giant panda, a black-and-white bear from the cold mountain forests of China that eats almost nothing but bamboo.
fact(The spare thumb): A panda has an extra "thumb" — not a finger at all but an enlarged wrist bone jutting out under its paw. This helps it hold and strip bamboo stems like you hold a pencil.
p: An adult eats 26-84 lb (12-38 kg) of bamboo a day, spending up to 15 hours munching.
check: What extra feature does the panda have that helps it hold bamboo?
+ An extra thumb-like bone
- A long, sticky tongue
- Webbed skin on each paw
hint: Look at the second paragraph and find the detail about the panda's paw.
explain: The panda has an enlarged wrist bone, which acts like an extra thumb to help hold bamboo.

--- 📊
p: The polar bear weighs about 992 lb, the grizzly bear about 551 lb, and the giant panda about 220 lb. The gray wolf is much lighter at about 88 lb, and the arctic fox is the smallest, at just 8 lb.
fact(Speed): The polar bear runs at 25 mph, while the gray wolf and grizzly bear both reach up to 35 mph for short sprints.
check: Which of the five is the heaviest?
- The grizzly bear
- The giant panda
+ The polar bear
hint: Look at the first paragraph and find the biggest number.
explain: The polar bear, at about 992 lb.

--- 🐾
p: The polar bear with its thick white fur, the grizzly bear with a powerful build, the giant panda with its distinctive black and white patches.
p: The gray wolf with its sleek coat, and the arctic fox that blends into the snow.
p: Five animals, each built for their own world, and all so different.

explain: Wolves have been seen heading straight for a moose 1.5 miles away, downwind and out of sight.

- Long hollow fangs that squirt out poison to stun and slow down prey
+ Sharp teeth shaped like blades that cut through hide, tendon, and bone
explain: Carnassials help wolves tear into their prey efficiently.

quiz: How far did the collared polar bear swim without stopping?
- 126 miles
+ 426 miles
- 926 miles
explain: She swam nine days straight across 426 miles of open Arctic sea.

quiz: How much bamboo does an adult giant panda eat each day?
- 10-25 lb (4.5-11 kg)
+ 26-84 lb (12-38 kg)

quiz: What is the hump on a grizzly's shoulders made of?

explain: The hump is solid muscle that powers the front legs for digging.

--- 🐾 @sloth
p: Meet the three-toed sloth, a shaggy leaf-eater that lives in the rainforest canopy and spends almost its entire life hanging upside down.
fact(The slowest): Top speed on the ground is about 0.15 mph (0.24 km/h). That is roughly 13 feet (4 m) in a minute. You cover that in two seconds.
p: Its fur can turn green with algae, making it nearly invisible in the trees. The claws are as long as your finger and perfect for hanging. A sloth climbs all the way down to the forest floor to poop only about once a week.

+ Once a week
- Once a month
- Once a day
hint: Look at the last sentence of the paragraph about its fur and claws.

--- 🐾 @naked-mole-rat
p: Next, meet the naked mole-rat, a nearly hairless rodent from East Africa that spends its whole life underground in a colony like an ant nest.
p: It is about as long as your finger and weighs about 1.2 oz (35 g), about the same as a large strawberry. The queen of the colony can grow much bigger, up to 2.8 oz (80 g).
term: colony = A group of animals that live together and work as one big family.
fact(The underground life): A naked mole-rat colony has one queen who is the only one to have babies. Everyone else digs, cleans, feeds, and defends the nest. Colonies can grow up to 300 animals.

--- 🐾 @pangolin
p: Next up is the pangolin, a shy night-time insect eater covered head to tail in hard scales.
fact(The armored ball): Threatened, a pangolin rolls into a ball so tight that even a big cat cannot get in. Predators have been filmed batting one around and eventually giving up.

+ It rolls into a tight ball
- It stabs attackers with sharp horns
hint: Look at the fact box about the armored ball.
explain: When threatened, a pangolin rolls into a tight ball, making it difficult for predators to get in.

--- 🐾 @platypus
p: Meet the platypus, an Australian river mammal with a soft duck-like bill, webbed feet, and a beaver’s tail. Only five kinds of mammal in the world lay eggs, and this is one of them.
fact(The egg-laying mammal): A platypus lays eggs, usually two small, leather-like ones. The mother curls around them in a burrow for about ten days until they hatch.
p: Its bill is packed with sensors that pick up the faint electricity of a shrimp's muscle movements. This is called electroreception and helps it hunt in muddy rivers with its eyes, ears, and nostrils all shut.
fact(Milk delivery): Platypus mothers have no nipples. Milk seeps out through the skin of the belly, pools in grooves in the fur, and the babies lap it up from there.

--- 🐾 @echidna
p: Meet the echidna, an ant-eating, spine-covered Australian mammal that lays a single egg into a pouch on its belly.
fact(The egg-laying pouch): An echidna lays one leathery egg straight into a pouch on her belly. Ten days later, a tiny blind and hairless baby called a puggle hatches inside the pouch. It stays there for two to three months.

- 2 months
+ 10 days
hint: Look at the box. It says how many days after the egg is laid the baby hatches.
explain: It takes 10 days for the puggle to hatch inside the pouch.

fact(Weight): The three-toed sloth weighs about 9 lb, the same as an echidna. A platypus is lighter at 3 lb, and a naked mole-rat weighs only about 1.2 oz.

- The three-toed sloth
- The naked mole-rat
+ The pangolin
hint: Look at the first paragraph and find the longest length.
explain: The pangolin is the longest at 2.6 ft.

--- 🐾
p: A three-toed sloth that moves slowly, a naked mole-rat that lives underground, a pangolin that curls into a ball, a platypus that lays eggs, and an echidna that has spines.
p: Five animals, each with its own way of living.

p: Start with the red kangaroo, the largest marsupial alive. A big male stands about 5 ft (1.5 m) tall and weighs about 146 lb (66 kg).
fact(The speed): In a short burst, a red kangaroo can hit about 35 mph (56 km/h), faster than any human sprinter who has ever lived.
p: Its legs are springs. Huge Achilles tendons in the lower legs stretch on landing and snap back on the next hop, so most of the energy from one hop gets recycled into the next. That is why it does not breathe harder as it speeds up.

+ Its legs are springs with enormous Achilles tendons

explain: The kangaroo's legs have enormous Achilles tendons that stretch when it lands and snap back to propel the next hop, recycling energy efficiently.

--- 🐾 @koala
p: Next, meet the koala, a thick-furred tree-living marsupial from eastern Australia. It eats almost nothing but eucalyptus leaves.
p: A koala's liver takes apart the toxins in eucalyptus leaves and flushes them out in its urine. The price is that eucalyptus leaves are tough, watery, and low in energy. So a koala spends up to 20 hours a day dozing or sitting perfectly still.
term: cecum = A pouch off the gut where millions of microbes live and break down leaf fiber.
fact(The gut bacteria): A joey's first solid food is pap, a soft green paste from its mother’s gut. Pap is full of living microbes that help the joey digest eucalyptus leaves.

--- 🐾 @wombat

p: Wombat droppings come out as cubes. Eighty to a hundred of them a night, and the wombat balances them on rocks and logs where other wombats will find them.
fact(Cubes in the wild): Scientists studied wombat intestines and found that stiff patches and soft patches in the last stretch squeeze the drying pellets into flat sides. A cube stays put on a rock. A ball rolls off.
check: Why do wombats produce cube-shaped droppings?

- To keep them warm inside the burrow
- To make them easier to eat

--- 🐾 @tasmanian-devil

fact(The strongest bite): For its size, no living mammal bites harder than the Tasmanian devil. Pound for pound, its jaws out-bite a lion's and it can crunch straight through leg bone.
p: Devils eat everything — skin, fur, organs, and bones. A dead wallaby can vanish overnight, leaving barely a stain on the grass.
p: When a devil is excited or upset, its ears turn red. Blood floods the thin bare skin there, and its mood becomes visible from across a clearing.

--- 🐾 @african-elephant

fact(The red sunscreen): Hippo skin oozes a thick red liquid called blood sweat — it's neither blood nor sweat. It acts as a natural sunscreen and antiseptic.
term: pigments = Substances that give color to something. In this case, hippo skin produces pigments that protect against UV light.
