=== astro-sun
idea: The sun is more than just a bright ball in the sky. Find out how its light travels and what happens when we look closely at its spots and colors.
game: Carrington Event = 1859 solar flare that sparked telegraph wires | Helium = Named after the sun before found on Earth | Parker Solar Probe = NASA's fastest, closest spacecraft to the sun | Sunspot = Cooler area that looks dark | Aurora = Sky glow seen during solar storms

--- 🕳️
p: The sun is so bright that looking straight at it can damage your eyes, but you won’t feel any pain.
fact: The retina in your eye has no pain nerves. So when sunlight burns it, nothing hurts while the damage happens.
try: Poke a small hole in a card and stand with your back to the sun. Let the light fall onto another card on the ground. You will see a tiny round image of the sun.
check: Why can staring at the sun do damage without any pain?
+ The retina has no pain nerves
- The sun is too bright
- Sunglasses block all the harmful rays
hint: Look at the fact box. It explains why you feel no pain.
explain: The retina in your eye has no pain nerves, so you won’t feel any pain even though the damage is happening.

--- 📏
p: The sun is huge, but we can work out just how big it is using simple geometry.
fact: The sun is about 864,000 miles across. If you lined up Earths edge to edge along that width, you would need 109 of them.
p: We know the distance to the sun because radar bounces off nearby planets, giving us precise distances in the solar system. Knowing how far away it is and how big it looks lets us measure its actual size.

--- ⏱️
p: Sunlight may seem to appear instantly, but it takes time to travel from the sun to Earth.
fact: Sunlight takes about 8 minutes 20 seconds to cross the 93 million miles (150 million km) to Earth. Both the distance and the speed of light are measured precisely.
check: How long does sunlight take to reach us?
- About 3 minutes 45 seconds
+ About 8 minutes 20 seconds
- About 14 minutes 30 seconds
hint: Look at the fact box on this page.
explain: Sunlight takes about 8 minutes 20 seconds to travel from the sun to Earth.

--- 🌡️
p: The sun has two temperatures that we talk about, but they are found in different ways.
fact: The surface of the sun is about 10,000 °F (5,500 °C), and that temperature is measured directly. But the center is about 27 million °F (15 million °C), worked out from how the sun rings and wobbles, like telling a cracked bell from a whole one.
check: The sun has a surface and a center. Which sun temperature is measured directly?
- The center
+ The surface
- Both temperatures
hint: The fact box tells you which temperature is measured directly and which one is worked out.
explain: The surface temperature of about 10,000 °F is measured directly. The center's temperature is reasoned from how the sun moves.

--- ⚫
term: sunspot = A cooler patch on the sun's surface. It looks black in a photograph, but it is not.
fact: A sunspot looks dark because it is cooler than the surface around it, about 6,300 °F compared to roughly 10,000 °F for the surface.
p: People have been drawing sunspots since telescopes came along in 1610. In 1843, Heinrich Schwabe noticed that the number of sunspots rises and falls in an eleven-year cycle after he spent seventeen years counting them nearly every day.
check: Why does a sunspot look dark?
+ It is cooler than the surface around it
- It is much hotter than the area around it
- It is a shadow made by a passing planet
hint: The fact box explains why a sunspot looks dark.
explain: A sunspot looks dark because it is cooler than the surface around it.

--- 🌀
p: The sun is not a solid ball like Earth. It moves differently at different spots.
fact: The equator of the sun turns around in about 25 days, but regions near the poles take closer to 35 days to complete one turn.
p: Sunspots close to the equator come back around sooner than spots farther north or south. A solid ball like Earth could never do that.

--- ⚡
p: On 1 September 1859, Richard Carrington was sketching sunspots when a patch of the sun flared white.
fact(The Carrington Event): About 17 hours later, telegraph wires across Europe and North America sparked. Operators on the Boston-to-Portland line unhooked their batteries but kept sending messages using the current from the sky.
p: Auroras were reported as far south as the Caribbean. Because so many people wrote it down at once, it is one of the best-documented things the sun has ever done.

--- 🌈
p: When the sun throws out a burst of particles, they light up the sky over the far north and far south.
fact: The sky glows green and red. That is the aurora.
try: Space weather forecasts are public. Search for the NOAA aurora forecast, and when the number called Kp climbs to 7 or higher, it is worth going somewhere dark and looking north.

--- 🚀
p: On December 24, 2024, NASA's Parker Solar Probe got really close to the sun.
fact(Close and fast): The probe flew 3.8 million miles above the sun's surface at about 430,000 miles per hour. Nothing made by people has ever gone faster or closer.
p: Two days later, it radioed back to say it was doing just fine.

--- 🟡
p: Helium was found in the sun before anyone found it on Earth. On August 18, 1868, Jules Janssen saw a strange yellow line during an eclipse.
fact: Norman Lockyer saw the same yellow line and named the new element after the Greek word for the sun. It turned up on Earth in volcanic gas at Vesuvius fourteen years later in 1882.
check: Where was helium found first?
- In party balloons
+ In the sun
- At Vesuvius
hint: The yellow line in sunlight was seen before it was found on Earth.
explain: Helium was first spotted as a yellow line in sunlight, not on Earth.

--- 📜
p: So why do we mark days and years?
fact(The written account says): The lights in the sky were set for signs and seasons, and for days and years. The sun marks a day as Earth turns once, and a year as it circles the sun.
p: Our calendar counts the sun. Whether it is on a wall or in a phone, it all comes back to the same star.

--- 🔭

try: Look at the edge of the lit part through binoculars or just your eyes. The craters stand up in relief along the terminator.

--- 🙈

fact: That day, the Soviet probe Luna 3 looped behind it and took 29 photographs. The pictures showed almost no dark plains like those on the near side.
p: The far side is not the dark side. It gets just as much sunlight as the side we see. When the moon looks black from Earth, the far side is in full daylight.
check: When the moon looks black from Earth, what is the far side doing?
- It looks black too
- It is hidden by Earth
+ It is in full daylight
hint: The far side gets just as much sunlight as the near side.
explain: The far side of the moon is in full daylight when the moon appears black from Earth.

--- 🎯
p: The moon always shows us the same face, but it is not perfectly still.
term: libration = The slight rocking and wobbling of the moon as it orbits Earth.
fact: Over time, we can see 59 percent of the moon’s surface because of this rocking and wobbling. Not half, a bit more than half.
p: Two things cause this wobble: the moon's orbit is not perfectly round, so it speeds up and slows down, and its axis is tilted about 6.7 degrees. This lets us peek slightly around each side, as well as over the top and under the bottom.

--- 🪞
p: Imagine if you could measure how fast something in space is moving with a laser.
fact: Apollo 11, 14, and 15 left special mirrors, called corner-cubes, on the moon. Observatories fire lasers at them to time the round trip. This shows the moon drifting away at about 1.5 inches each year, which is roughly as fast as your fingernails grow.
check: How fast is the moon drifting away?
+ About 1.5 inches a year
- About 9 inches a year
- About 2 feet each year
hint: The fact box gives the exact speed.
explain: The moon drifts away at about 1.5 inches each year, which is as fast as a fingernail grows.

--- 〰️
p: Apollo crews left seismometers on the moon. Between 1972 and 1977, they recorded 28 shallow moonquakes.
fact: The strongest was about magnitude 5.5, and some kept shaking for over ten minutes. Dry cracked rock with no water in it rings on instead of settling down.
p: Nobody has drilled deep into the moon, so what we know about its middle is worked out from seismometers and the laser wobble.
check: How is the inside of the moon known?
- Seen directly by a robot sent down a very deep hole
+ Worked out from seismometers and the laser wobble
- Known from X-ray pictures taken from the Earth
hint: The last paragraph explains how scientists figure it out.
explain: Seismometers recorded the ground shaking, and mirrors let lasers measure the moon's wobble. Together, they give clues about what is inside.

--- 📜
p: The moon keeps track of time.
fact(The written account says): In Genesis, the lesser light is set over the night to mark signs and seasons. The moon goes from new to full and back in about 29 and a half days. That is where the month came from.
